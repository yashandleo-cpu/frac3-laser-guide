FRAC3 Laser Skin Remodeling: A Plain Guide for First-Timers

How it works, who it suits, and what the weeks after a session look like.

Most people hear about FRAC3 from a friend whose skin suddenly looks calmer and more even, and who says there was almost no downtime. That is a fair summary. FRAC3 is one of the gentler laser options, and it is often chosen by people who want steady improvement without peeling or days hidden at home. This guide explains it without the jargon.

What FRAC3 actually is

FRAC3 is a setting on an Nd:YAG laser. Instead of treating the whole surface evenly, it sends very short pulses of energy that are absorbed in tiny, scattered spots beneath the skin. The top layer is left intact, which is why it is called "non-ablative". The small heated spots prompt the skin to repair itself and lay down fresh collagen over the following weeks.

In one line: the laser works under the surface and leaves the outside of the skin alone, so recovery is short.

What people use it for

  • Dull or uneven skin tone
  • Enlarged pores and oily, breakout-prone skin
  • Mild redness and small visible vessels
  • Early fine lines and loss of bounce
  • Light pigmentation, as part of a wider plan

It is not the tool for deep acne scars or heavy sun damage. Stronger resurfacing lasers suit those better, with more downtime in exchange.

What a session feels like

  1. Assessment. Your skin type and any pigment are checked so the energy level can be set safely.
  2. Treatment. The handpiece is passed over clean skin for about half an hour. Most people describe warmth and light snapping. Numbing cream is usually not needed.
  3. Straight after. Skin can look pink and feel warm, a little like after exercise. This normally settles within hours, sometimes a couple of days.

How many sessions, and how far apart

StageTypical pattern
First course3 to 5 sessions
Gap between sessionsAbout 4 to 6 weeks
UpkeepA lighter session every 3 to 6 months, if wanted

Results build slowly. Collagen takes weeks to form, so the change after one visit is usually subtle. Photos taken before the first session are the most honest way to judge progress.

A note for medium and deeper skin tones

Any laser can trigger dark marks in skin that tans easily, including many East Asian and South Asian skin types. With FRAC3 the risk is lower than with harsher lasers, but it is not zero. A careful provider will use lower energy, may ask you to prepare your skin for a few weeks first, and may do a small test spot before treating the whole face. If nobody asks about your skin type or tanning history, treat that as a warning sign.

Looking after your skin afterwards

  • Day of treatment: skip makeup and hot water on the face.
  • First three days: sunscreen SPF 50 every morning, plain moisturiser, no scrubs or acids.
  • First week: avoid saunas, hot yoga and strong sun.
  • After two weeks: bring your usual products back one at a time.

Who should wait

FRAC3 is usually postponed if you are pregnant or breastfeeding, have an active skin infection or a fresh sunburn, or take medicines that make skin sensitive to light. Tell your provider about every medicine and supplement you use.

Questions worth asking at a consultation

  • Who performs the treatment, and what is their training?
  • Is the device licensed by Health Canada?
  • How will you adjust the settings for my skin type?
  • What result is realistic for my concern, and after how many sessions?
